## HS01: provider, region and isolation

DigitalOcean's [regional matrix](https://docs.digitalocean.com/platform/regional-availability/) lists app VMs and PostgreSQL in NYC3 and SFO3. Keep app and DB in the **same selected region and private network**. There is no measured latency, contractual US-only data map or live capacity observation in this review. A user's time zone is not evidence of operator or customer location. Compare region proximity only when the actual people and intended service footprint are known.

Before a future selection, document billing/account ownership and eligibility; exact Linux image/CPU/RAM/disk; DB edition/version/storage; private operator/deployment path; registry availability; and every data location for objects, KMS/secrets, workflows, independent backups, logs and support access. US app/DB placement alone does not establish US-only processing or backup residency. Cross-provider transfer, outbound workflow/provider access and future signed callback reachability need an explicit network/cost map under HS03/HS06/HS07. The developer workstation remains an operator client, not a hosting dependency.

One app VM and one DB primary are staging failure domains. They do not prove production HA, a 99.9% target or cross-region recovery. Production vendor/topology remains a separate decision. If NYC3 is unavailable or unsuitable, stop and review SFO3 or another topology, prices, data locations, latency assumptions and recovery path; do not silently move the DB or lower controls.

## HS02: compatibility findings

| Area | Evidence and gap | Required disposition before acceptance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Version and patch lifecycle | Local images and dump/restore tools pin **PostgreSQL 18.6**. The provider documents major-version choice with the latest supported minor and [automatic maintenance](https://docs.digitalocean.com/products/databases/postgresql/details/features/) | Catalog support for major 18 does not prove 18.6 or the ability to hold it. Record exact offered/observed version and maintenance behavior. Any patch-policy change needs a separately reviewed compatibility matrix and local tests; no downgrade or guard relaxation here |
| Bootstrap database owner | [Bootstrap](../../scripts/database/bootstrap.ts) accepts only `local_harness` or the exact app/CMS migrator as initial DB owner, refuses foreign table owners, then transfers DB/public-schema ownership | A default provider-owned DB is refused. Define how fresh dedicated app/CMS DBs acquire exact owners without importing local superuser credentials or targeting provider defaults |
| Roles and memberships | Bootstrap requires 19 exact scoped role names, restrictive flags, and **no membership in either direction** on replay. [PostgreSQL 18](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/role-attributes.html) gives a non-superuser creator an automatic admin membership that it cannot itself remove | An ordinary restricted-admin `CREATEROLE` path conflicts with replay invariants. This is a documented/static inference, not a measured DigitalOcean failure. Investigate provider-supported role creation and cleanup; never ignore memberships or elevate runtime roles to obtain a pass |
| Ownership and default privileges | Bootstrap changes ownership and `ALTER DEFAULT PRIVILEGES FOR ROLE`; ordinary [ownership transfer](https://www.postgresql.org/docs/18/sql-alterdatabase.html) requires `CREATEDB` and ability to `SET ROLE` to the new owner | Show the complete scoped administrative path, re-run bootstrap/migrations and verify ACLs, RLS, defaults and cross-DB denial. Temporary administrative grants must not survive as undeclared project-role membership |
| Managed editions and names | [Provider user guidance](https://docs.digitalocean.com/products/databases/postgresql/how-to/modify-user-privileges/) limits Advanced-edition user names to lowercase letters, digits, hyphens and periods; our 19 names contain underscores. It also notes new Advanced users have `CREATEDB` | Advanced is not an assumed drop-in growth path. Confirm the exact SQL/API behavior before any future use. Do not rename project roles, introduce a mapping or weaken capability checks in this review |
| Capacity | [Published limits](https://docs.digitalocean.com/products/databases/postgresql/details/limits/) list 97 available backends for 4 GiB; 2 GiB lists 47 | Catalog headroom is only a screen. Reconcile actual raw/reserved/unavailable slots, every process/pool, external client and operational reserve. No hosted sizing is accepted |
| Growth and HA | [Current plan notice](https://docs.digitalocean.com/products/databases/) limits new Standard plans to 4 GiB and no standby/read-only nodes for previously unused accounts from October 15, and all accounts from November 30, 2026 | Recheck actual account/edition rules. A larger/HA service needs a new compatibility, migration and cost proposal; do not infer that a price-table row is available or that an edition change is in-place |
| Restore and independent custody | Provider [restore documentation](https://docs.digitalocean.com/products/databases/postgresql/how-to/restore-from-backups/) describes seven-day backups and a new primary for restore; destroying a cluster also destroys its provider backups | Provider recovery alone is not independent custody. Preserve separately encrypted copies, nonempty signed journals, key continuity, quarantine and scoped app/CMS restore/replay; reprice retained bytes and new-cluster allowance |
| Other managed services | [RDS documentation](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AmazonRDS/latest/UserGuide/Appendix.PostgreSQL.CommonDBATasks.Roles.rds_superuser.html) distinguishes `rds_superuser` from PostgreSQL superuser | AWS needs its own bootstrap/ownership/membership/restore contract and quote. Do not transfer S2 assumptions or pass local superuser evidence off as RDS evidence |

[Managed cluster creation](https://docs.digitalocean.com/products/databases/postgresql/how-to/create/) documents latest-minor provisioning and permanent edition choice. That is why the growth and patch questions must be resolved before selection. Inventory required collation/timezone/encoding and [extensions](https://docs.digitalocean.com/products/databases/postgresql/details/supported-extensions/) from the actual schema; listing an extension is not installation authority or a reason to add one.

The current database CLI, [recovery runner](../../scripts/verify/recovery.mjs), runtime factory and provider/callback configuration are deliberately local-only. A generic hosted connection URI is not a supported deployment mode. The earlier SP01–SP08 proofs demonstrate local refusals and recovery, not that these provider-specific administration paths already exist. Keep all local guards intact.

## Capacity, private access and recovery conditions

Preserve the original **45 usable-slot single-host / 62 overlapping-host proposals**. They describe their declared hosted shape, not the complete current proof fixture. [SP06](P1-PR-027-local-capacity-preflight.md) required 68 usable slots; [SP07](P1-PR-027-local-observation-preflight.md) required **69**, with 75 raw minus five reserved = 70 usable in its isolated fixture. Neither fixture total is a hosted performance result. A future hosted observer/registry/agent/worker composition must recalculate maxima; no subtraction of observed idle sockets, double-counting of provider reservations, automatic PgBouncer dependency or autoscaling assumption.

Private network routing does not authenticate a DB server. The provider's [security guidance](https://docs.digitalocean.com/products/databases/postgresql/how-to/secure/) supports trusted-source restrictions and full TLS verification. A future authorized proof must deny an unrelated network source, reject wrong CA/hostname/expired certificate, verify restrictive role/schema access, and keep migrations/restore on a reviewed direct path. Do not copy `sslmode=require` as sufficient identity verification. Public defaults must never become the intended staging access policy.

The [notification policy](https://docs.digitalocean.com/products/databases/postgresql/details/notifications/) includes automatic emails to a team destination that cannot be disabled/customized. No account or cluster is created here. Future procurement must explicitly resolve and authorize unavoidable provider account notifications and destinations; the current **no-notifications** instruction cannot be met by pretending those effects do not exist. It remains in force now, including no actual recipient probes or quote-request outreach.

Retain existing RTO ≤ four hours and RPO ≤ 24 hours; an RPO ≤ 15 minutes claim still requires accepted PITR evidence. Retention windows remain engineering proposals subject to policy review. Do not substitute seven-day provider backups for proposed 30-day independent copies or omit the external-journal recovery after DB restore. Unstaffed time can violate event-to-recovery targets even if a provider operates the DB. The [operator plan](../operations/staging-operator-responsibilities.md) explicitly leaves coverage unconfirmed; a managed-service SLA is not this project's staffing or recovery acceptance.
